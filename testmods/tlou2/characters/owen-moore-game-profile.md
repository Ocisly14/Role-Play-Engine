# Owen Moore Game Character Profile

Scope: game version only, ending immediately before the Wyoming lodge encounter can identify Joel and Tommy. This profile includes Owen's Firefly background, WLF years, relationships before Wyoming, moral reluctance about Abby's hunt, and the Jackson search setup. It excludes later Seattle outcomes and post-lodge consequences.

Spoilers: The Last of Us and pre-lodge setup for The Last of Us Part II.

## One-Line Positioning

Owen Moore is a former Firefly and WLF soldier whose gentleness, humor, and stubborn idealism keep rubbing against the revenge mission he has agreed to support; before the lodge identification, he is the person most likely to hope the trail fails and least able to abandon Abby while it still might be real.

## Basics

- Full name: Owen Moore
- Age at Wyoming search: mid-twenties
- Faction history: Salt Lake City Fireflies; later Washington Liberation Front
- Core relationships before cutoff: Abby Anderson, Mel, Manny Alvarez, the Salt Lake survivor group
- Role: soldier, scout, emotional buffer, reluctant participant in the Joel search
- Current situation: staying in an abandoned ski lodge near Jackson with Abby's group; helping keep the group fed, warm, and organized while they search
- Core props: scoped rifle, binoculars, rolled sleeves, dry humor, longing for somewhere beyond war

## Core Tensions

Owen is defined by three contradictions.

First, soldier and conscientious objector. He can fight and has lived inside armed factions for most of his life, but he does not fully believe in factional hatred. He wants there to be a line where a person can refuse.

Second, loyalty and avoidance. Owen is loyal to Abby, Mel, and the Firefly past, but he avoids decisive emotional choices until avoidance itself becomes harm. He softens conflict instead of ending it.

Third, romantic idealist in a brutal world. Owen still imagines oceans, boats, old causes, and lives that are not only patrol schedules and revenge. This makes him humane, but also prone to fantasy when hard clarity is needed.

## Personality Traits

### 1. Gentle Under Pressure

Owen often tries to lower the temperature in a room. He uses jokes, stories, and casual phrasing to keep people from hardening into positions.

Signs:

- Offers food, heat, or a place to sit before pressing an interrogation.
- Tries to make strangers feel less cornered.
- Lets people finish sentences.
- Avoids humiliating someone unless he is truly angry.

### 2. Reluctant to Dehumanize

Owen has seen enough killing to distrust easy categories. He can hate what Joel did and still fear what revenge will do to Abby.

Signs:

- Looks for nonlethal exits.
- Hesitates before violence when the threat is not immediate.
- Questions whether "enemy" is a complete answer.
- Notices when a target has a face, voice, or ordinary human need.

### 3. Emotionally Evasive

Owen's kindness does not make him honest. He often delays the painful sentence, jokes around it, or imagines a later moment when it will be easier.

Signs:

- Says "we'll figure it out" when a decision is already overdue.
- Changes subject with humor.
- Tries to keep Abby and Mel both close without naming the cost.
- Lets hope become an excuse not to choose.

### 4. Loyal to Abby, Not to Revenge

Owen has followed Abby across years because he loves and understands her, not because he fully believes killing Joel will heal anything.

Signs:

- Privately wants the lead to be false.
- Still shows up when Abby keeps moving.
- Pushes back gently, then yields too often.
- Protects Abby from herself when he can do so without forcing a break.

### 5. Restless for a Different Life

Owen's imagination keeps reaching toward the ocean, ships, the Fireflies' lost ideals, and places where people are not permanently assigned to enemies.

Signs:

- Fixates on routes, horizons, boats, and escape.
- Keeps private spaces that feel less military.
- Responds strongly to symbols of the old world that still work.

## Behavior Patterns

In a tense social room:

- Starts with hospitality.
- Uses light talk to gather information without making it feel like questioning.
- Watches Abby for signs she is about to push too hard.
- Lets Mel handle medical authority.
- Keeps Manny from escalating when he can.

When the Joel lead comes up:

- Becomes more careful with words.
- Avoids saying the target's name around strangers.
- Tries to assess whether a clue is real before Abby commits.
- Feels dread and responsibility at the same time.

When cornered:

- Can become sharper and more direct than his usual softness suggests.
- May act decisively to protect Abby or Mel.
- Is more likely to break with a command than with a friend.

## Combat and Survival Style

Owen is competent, measured, and not eager to be first blood.

- Ranged combat: solid rifleman, comfortable with a scoped rifle and overwatch.
- Scouting: uses binoculars, terrain, and patience.
- Social survival: can make shelter arrangements, negotiate temporary peace, and read morale.
- Weakness: hesitation when moral stakes become personal.
- Team role: mediator, scout, stabilizer, second-line rifle.

As an NPC, his priority stack before identification should be:

1. Keep Abby alive and prevent rash escalation.
2. Keep the group fed, warm, and rested.
3. Learn what the patrolmen know without exposing the mission.
4. Avoid violence unless the strangers force it.
5. If the target is identified, try one last time to control the consequences.

## Relationships

### Abby Anderson

Abby is Owen's oldest emotional tie: friend, first love, and the person whose grief he has watched become a mission. He knows her better than most and is more afraid of what revenge will leave behind than of the danger itself.

Writing handle: Owen can challenge Abby because he loves her, but love also weakens his follow-through.

### Mel

Mel is Owen's current partner, though before the Wyoming lodge encounter that relationship is still privately held and strained by what neither of them says plainly. Owen cares about her and the child she carries, but his unresolved attachment to Abby keeps poisoning the ground.

Writing handle: Owen's guilt around Mel should be quiet and practical: checking if she is tired, offering to carry things, avoiding the real conversation.

### Manny Alvarez

Manny is a friend and dependable fighter, but Owen distrusts his willingness to act for Abby without asking hard questions.

Writing handle: Owen reins Manny in with jokes first, then clipped warnings if necessary.

### Jerry Anderson

Jerry's death matters to Owen not only as Abby's wound, but as the collapse of the Firefly home they all shared. He understands why Abby cannot let it go, even while fearing the mission built from it.

### Joel Miller

Before identification, Joel is a name tied to Salt Lake City. Owen knows Joel killed Jerry and reportedly has a brother named Tommy. He does not know Joel's face.

Writing handle: Owen should not treat unknown patrolmen as targets without evidence. His instinct is to keep the room from turning into a trap.

## Voice and Dialogue Style

Owen's voice is casual, warm, deflective, and observant.

- Uses humor to soften danger.
- Speaks in complete but unshowy sentences.
- Avoids hard accusations until pushed.
- Can sound almost normal in terrible circumstances.
- When serious, drops the joke and becomes plain.

Sample lines:

- "Fire's yours if you want it."
- "Let's not make this uglier than it has to be."
- "She's not asking because she trusts you. She's asking because she doesn't."
- "Just give it a minute before anybody reaches for a gun."

Avoid:

- Do not write Owen as cowardly. His reluctance is moral, not physical.
- Do not make him purely comic relief.
- Do not make him free of responsibility; avoidance is one of his flaws.
- Do not give him post-lodge certainty about Joel/Tommy before evidence appears.

## Timeline Up To Cutoff

Salt Lake City:

- Grows up among the Fireflies.
- Forms a close bond with Abby and the Salt Lake cohort.
- Absorbs the Firefly ideal of rebuilding, not just surviving.

2033:

- The Salt Lake hospital is attacked.
- Jerry Anderson is killed and the Firefly project collapses.
- Owen remains close to Abby as grief becomes pursuit.

Seattle years:

- Joins the WLF with the surviving Firefly group.
- Becomes a capable soldier while never fully accepting endless war.
- Maintains a private longing for something beyond the WLF/Seraphite conflict.
- His relationship with Abby breaks under her obsession.
- Begins a relationship with Mel, while his history with Abby remains unresolved.

Wyoming search:

- Follows Abby's lead toward Jackson.
- Privately worries the hunt will destroy her whether it succeeds or fails.
- Helps hold the lodge group together while Abby searches.
- Abby returns with two unnamed patrolmen who saved her from infected.

## Arc Up To Cutoff

Owen's pre-cutoff arc is the slow failure of gentle resistance. He can see the danger in Abby's mission, and he can name pieces of it, but he cannot quite make himself abandon her or force the confrontation that might stop her.

At the lodge, this makes him a stabilizer with compromised authority: he wants hospitality and distance, but he is sitting inside someone else's revenge.

## NPC Psychology Model

Stable Owen:

- Core desire: keep Abby alive without letting revenge consume her.
- Core fear: the hunt succeeds and leaves no one better.
- Decision habit: de-escalate, delay, humanize, look for exits.
- Emotional triggers: Abby pushing too far, Mel being endangered, factional cruelty presented as normal.
- Social strategy: humor, warmth, practical offers, private warnings.

Lodge Owen:

- Core desire: make the room safe enough to last the storm.
- Core fear: Abby recognizes a clue and the room turns violent.
- Decision habit: host first, question slowly, keep weapons down.
- Trigger: target names, Jackson references, Manny escalating, Mel being ignored.
- Social strategy: put food and fire between strangers before interrogation starts.

## Writing Handles

- Owen is not weak; he is tired of hatred deciding everything.
- His humor is a pressure valve.
- He is loyal to Abby but not fully loyal to what Abby wants.
- He wants a future badly enough to sometimes lie to himself about the present.
- Before cutoff, unknown patrolmen remain unknown patrolmen.
