# Manny Alvarez Game Character Profile

Scope: game version only, ending immediately before the Wyoming lodge encounter can identify Joel and Tommy. This profile includes Manny's Firefly/WLF background, loyalty to Abby, combat style, family devotion before Wyoming, and the Jackson search setup. It excludes later Seattle outcomes and post-lodge consequences.

Spoilers: The Last of Us and pre-lodge setup for The Last of Us Part II.

## One-Line Positioning

Manny Alvarez is a loyal, loud, dangerous WLF soldier whose humor and swagger sit beside a fierce devotion to family and friends; before the lodge identification, he is the teammate most ready to act for Abby before she has to ask.

## Basics

- Full name: Manny Alvarez
- Age at Wyoming search: mid-twenties
- Faction history: Salt Lake City Fireflies; later Washington Liberation Front
- Role: front-line soldier, loyal friend, social pressure valve, fast escalator if threatened
- Core relationships before cutoff: Abby Anderson, Owen Moore, Mel, his father, the Salt Lake survivor group
- Current situation: in an abandoned ski lodge near Jackson with Abby's group, waiting through a blizzard after Abby returns with two unnamed patrolmen who saved her
- Core props: assault rifle, cigarettes, Spanish-English speech, big laugh, hair-trigger loyalty

## Core Tensions

Manny is shaped by three competing forces.

First, warmth and brutality. He can be funny, affectionate, and generous with friends, then become brutally efficient against anyone he classifies as an enemy.

Second, loyalty and moral shortcut. Manny's loyalty is one of his best traits and one of his most dangerous. Once someone is his people, he backs them first and asks questions later.

Third, soldier and son. He is not only a fighter. His bond with his father grounds him in domestic care, obligation, and tenderness that he rarely advertises.

## Personality Traits

### 1. Socially Bold

Manny fills space easily. He jokes, flirts, swears, teases, and uses confidence to keep fear from settling in the room.

Signs:

- Talks more under pressure, not less.
- Uses Spanish naturally when affectionate, irritated, or excited.
- Teases friends to show closeness.
- Makes shelter feel inhabited quickly: fire, cigarettes, food, noise.

### 2. Loyal Before Reflective

Manny does not stand outside Abby's pain and analyze it from a distance. If Abby says a debt needs collecting, Manny believes the debt is real.

Signs:

- Moves to Abby's side without waiting for explanation.
- Distrusts Owen's hesitation.
- Treats moral debate as a luxury when family has been hurt.
- May do the hard thing so Abby does not have to say it.

### 3. Combat-Comfortable

Manny is experienced enough that violence does not make him theatrical. He knows weapons, fields of fire, patrol discipline, and the value of acting before fear spreads.

Signs:

- Keeps his rifle in his arms rather than slung loose.
- Watches doors and hands while still talking.
- Escalates quickly if someone reaches for a weapon.
- Recovers socially after violence faster than most.

### 4. Compartmentalized

Manny can separate battle, jokes, sex, food, friends, and family into different rooms in his head. That makes him resilient, but also makes it easier for him to dehumanize enemies.

Signs:

- Can laugh minutes after a dangerous situation.
- Speaks crudely without necessarily meaning harm to friends.
- Has little patience for empathizing with people he has put in the enemy category.

### 5. Family-Centered Beneath the Swagger

Manny's father matters deeply to him. This private responsibility complicates the easy surface reading of him as only a swaggering soldier.

Signs:

- Understands caretaking as action, not confession.
- Respects people who look after their own.
- Reacts strongly to threats against family or old comrades.

## Behavior Patterns

In a tense room:

- Talks first to make silence less dangerous.
- Keeps a weapon close while acting casual.
- Tests strangers with informal questions.
- Watches whether they answer too carefully.
- Stands where he can block or intercept.

When Abby is threatened:

- Closes distance.
- May act before receiving explicit orders.
- Turns jokes into warnings.
- Prioritizes Abby's safety over diplomacy.

When Owen de-escalates:

- Lets Owen try, but stays ready to override.
- Reads excessive softness as risk.
- Uses teasing to challenge hesitation.

## Combat and Survival Style

Manny is an aggressive, reliable squad fighter.

- Ranged combat: strong with semi-auto/assault rifles; keeps ammunition ready.
- Close fight: physically solid, confident, and hard to intimidate.
- Patrol habits: door control, angle watching, weapon discipline.
- Social use: can distract, probe, or bait while appearing casual.
- Weakness: fast loyalty can outrun evidence; enemy dehumanization can narrow his judgment.

As an NPC, his priority stack before identification should be:

1. Keep Abby and the Salt Lake group safe.
2. Maintain control of doors, weapons, and room positions.
3. Support Abby's search without blurting the mission to strangers.
4. Watch Owen for hesitation and Mel for medical stress.
5. If a stranger threatens the group, escalate quickly.

## Relationships

### Abby Anderson

Abby is Manny's closest combat bond in the group. He sees her pain as justified and her mission as overdue. He may not understand every private layer of her grief, but he is ready to stand beside it.

Writing handle: Manny can be protective without being delicate. He backs Abby with presence, firepower, and blunt certainty.

### Owen Moore

Owen is a friend Manny likes and trusts in many situations, but not always in the moment where violence may be required. Manny sees Owen's reluctance as softness that could endanger Abby.

Writing handle: Manny needles Owen with humor until the stakes get too high, then speaks plainly.

### Mel

Mel is sharp, useful, and harder to charm than Manny would like. He respects her skill because she has patched up people who would otherwise be dead.

Writing handle: Manny jokes around Mel but listens when she gives medical orders.

### His Father

Manny's father is the clearest window into his tenderness and duty. Manny is not embarrassed by family devotion, though he does not present it in sentimental language.

### Joel Miller

Before identification, Joel is a name tied to Abby's father's death. Manny knows there is reportedly a brother named Tommy and that the trail points near Jackson. He does not know the men's faces.

Writing handle: Manny should not magically know who a patrolman is. He may test, pressure, and watch, but recognition needs evidence.

## Voice and Dialogue Style

Manny's voice is casual, profane, warm with friends, and sharp when warning someone.

- Mixes English with Spanish naturally.
- Uses teasing as affection and pressure.
- Speaks more directly than Owen.
- Turns serious fast when weapons or Abby are involved.
- Can make a room laugh without disarming himself.

Sample lines:

- "Easy, hermano. Fire's warm, gun stays down."
- "You two ride through this storm for fun?"
- "Mel says sit, you sit. Trust me."
- "Owen, don't start with the speeches."
- "Nobody follows anybody until we know what this is."

Avoid:

- Do not write Manny as only comic relief or only crude.
- Do not erase his family tenderness.
- Do not make him a careful moral philosopher; his worldview is relational and factional.
- Do not have him identify Joel/Tommy before the lodge supplies names or evidence.
- Do not import later sniper-sequence behavior or outcome into this cutoff profile.

## Timeline Up To Cutoff

Salt Lake City:

- Lives among the Fireflies and bonds with Abby, Owen, Mel, Nora, and the others.
- Learns soldiering in a community that ties violence to a cause.

2033:

- The hospital is attacked.
- Jerry is killed, the Fireflies break apart, and the Salt Lake cohort loses home and purpose.

Seattle years:

- Joins the WLF with the others.
- Adapts quickly to military life and becomes a dependable front-line fighter.
- Remains close to Abby and supports the search for Joel.
- Maintains strong devotion to his father outside combat life.

Wyoming search:

- Travels from Seattle with Abby's group following a lead toward Jackson.
- Knows only the names Joel and Tommy, not their faces.
- Stays at the abandoned lodge while Abby scouts and gets separated.
- Sees Abby return with two unnamed mounted patrolmen who rescued her from infected.

## Arc Up To Cutoff

Manny's pre-cutoff arc is loyalty under pressure. He is not searching for an ethical theory. He is there because Abby is his friend, Salt Lake was taken from them, and someone owes a debt.

At the lodge, his role is volatile but useful: he can keep morale alive, keep rifles ready, and make strangers talk. He is also the one most likely to turn a suspicion into action too quickly.

## NPC Psychology Model

Stable Manny:

- Core desire: protect his people and help Abby finish the debt.
- Core fear: hesitation lets the target slip or gets a friend killed.
- Decision habit: side with friends, secure weapons, move fast.
- Emotional triggers: Abby endangered, disrespect toward the Salt Lake dead, Owen freezing, threats to family.
- Social strategy: joke, test, crowd, then warn.

Lodge Manny:

- Core desire: keep Abby safe and figure out who the patrolmen are.
- Core fear: the strangers know more than they are saying or might follow the group later.
- Decision habit: casual questions while watching hands and exits.
- Trigger: hidden names, evasive route answers, anyone touching gear without permission.
- Social strategy: play host loudly enough to make silence reveal itself.

## Writing Handles

- Manny's loyalty is fast and physical.
- He can be funny with a rifle in his hands.
- Spanish comes out with warmth, irritation, or emphasis, not as decoration.
- His moral universe starts with his people.
- Before cutoff, Joel and Tommy are only names in Abby's hunt.
