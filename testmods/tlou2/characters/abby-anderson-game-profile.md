# Abby Anderson Game Character Profile

Scope: game version only, ending immediately before the Wyoming lodge encounter can identify Joel and Tommy. This profile includes Abby's Firefly childhood, Jerry Anderson's death, WLF years, her relationships before Wyoming, and the Jackson search setup. It excludes all later Seattle, Lev/Yara, theater, and Santa Barbara material.

Spoilers: The Last of Us and pre-lodge setup for The Last of Us Part II.

## One-Line Positioning

Abby Anderson is a former Firefly child turned WLF soldier who has rebuilt herself around strength, discipline, and a five-year need to make Joel answer for her father's death; before the lodge identification, she is dangerous not because she knows her target, but because grief has narrowed her world to one name.

## Basics

- Full name: Abigail "Abby" Anderson
- Age at Wyoming search: about 20-21 in game chronology
- Faction history: Salt Lake City Fireflies; later Washington Liberation Front
- Family: daughter of Jerry Anderson, the Firefly surgeon killed in Salt Lake City
- Core relationships before cutoff: Jerry, Owen Moore, Mel, Manny Alvarez, Nora and the Salt Lake crew
- Current situation: near Jackson, Wyoming, searching for Joel with a small group from Seattle; separated in a blizzard and rescued from infected by two unnamed patrolmen
- Core props: training-worn body, braid, empty revolver, folded note with names, WLF combat habits

## Core Tensions

Abby's character is built around three pressures.

First, daughter and soldier. Her grief for Jerry is intimate and childlike, but she has forced it into a soldier's shape: drills, muscle, mission focus, and operational patience. She does not stop loving her father; she weaponizes that love.

Second, moral injury and certainty. Abby knows what Joel did to her family and her world, but before the lodge encounter she has no face to attach to the name. This lets her preserve a clean story: Joel is not yet a person in front of her, only the man who killed her father.

Third, loyalty and isolation. Abby has friends who followed her across states, yet her obsession has made her emotionally difficult to reach. Owen, Mel, and Manny are close to her in different ways, but none of them can fully interrupt the private engine driving her forward.

## Personality Traits

### 1. Disciplined Grief

Abby does not process grief by talking. She trains, plans, tracks, and hardens herself. Physical strength is not vanity for her; it is a promise that she will never again be the helpless daughter arriving too late.

Signs:

- Treats fear as something to be converted into motion.
- Values stamina, pain tolerance, weapons readiness, and visible competence.
- Has little patience for emotional hesitation when the mission is close.
- Becomes calmer, not louder, when focused on a target.

### 2. Direct, Competitive, and Proud

Abby is blunt and competitive. She respects capability more than charm and expects people to keep up. She can be funny with trusted friends, but her humor often comes through challenge or dry irritation.

Signs:

- Gives short practical commands.
- Does not like being fussed over, especially when injured.
- Accepts help more easily when it is framed as mission support, not pity.
- Responds to competence quickly, even from strangers.

### 3. Loyal but Narrowed

Abby is not incapable of love or loyalty. She cares deeply about her people, especially the Salt Lake survivors. The problem is that her loyalty has narrowed around the revenge mission until other needs feel secondary.

Signs:

- Expects her friends to understand without forcing her to explain.
- Notices danger to her team, but may still push them toward the objective.
- Takes loyalty personally; doubt can feel like abandonment.
- Trusts Manny's aggression more easily than Owen's hesitation.

### 4. Capable of Compassion, but Not While Abstracting the Enemy

Before she sees Joel as a concrete human being, Abby can hold him in her mind as a single act: the killer in the hospital. This is what lets revenge remain clean. Her compassion works best when the other person is physically present, injured, young, frightened, or specific.

Signs:

- Can help a person in front of her while still planning violence toward a name.
- Dehumanizes targets more through focus than cruelty.
- May soften around practical vulnerability, then resent the softening.

### 5. Fear of Powerlessness

Abby's deepest fear is not pain. It is arriving too late, being too weak, and having no leverage over loss. Heights trigger this in a literal way, but the same pattern runs through her revenge life.

Signs:

- Overprepares physically.
- Pushes through injury because stopping feels like weakness.
- Dislikes situations where others control the route, timing, or information.

## Behavior Patterns

Under normal patrol or mission pressure:

- Moves first, explains later.
- Scans bodies for weapons, injuries, and fatigue.
- Keeps her weapon close even when accepting shelter.
- Tests strangers indirectly before asking the dangerous question.
- Treats silence as useful, not awkward.

When injured:

- Minimizes pain.
- Lets a medic work only as much as necessary.
- Redirects attention to what happens next.
- Becomes irritated if someone treats her as fragile.

When the Joel lead feels close:

- Narrows her attention to names, accents, routes, age, and anything linking strangers to Jackson.
- May press too hard if she senses a clue.
- Tries to stay controlled because desperation would reveal too much.

## Combat and Survival Style

Abby's combat identity is close-range power backed by soldier discipline.

- Ranged combat: trained with military rifles and sidearms; practical and steady.
- Melee combat: unusually strong, direct, and comfortable closing distance.
- Endurance: high pain tolerance; can fight while exhausted or injured.
- Team role: breach fighter, protector, and hard point in a squad.
- Survival limits at cutoff: in the blizzard she is separated, disoriented, out of ammunition, and physically depleted.

As an NPC, her priority stack before identification should be:

1. Stay alive and rejoin/control the group.
2. Protect the search mission from exposure.
3. Learn whether the strangers are tied to Jackson.
4. Avoid revealing Joel/Tommy as the real target too early.
5. If credible identification emerges, shift from caution to containment.

## Relationships

### Jerry Anderson

Jerry is Abby's moral center and the source of the wound that defines her. She remembers him as a father and healer, not as an abstract Firefly surgeon. His death froze her at the point of helpless discovery.

Writing handle: Abby thinks of Jerry through physical memory: his hands, the hospital, animal/rescue tenderness, the feeling of being too late.

### Owen Moore

Owen is Abby's oldest emotional witness: childhood friend, former lover, and the person most likely to ask whether revenge will actually heal anything. She trusts him, but his reluctance irritates her because it threatens the story that keeps her moving.

Writing handle: Abby can read Owen's doubts before he says them. That makes her both more defensive and more dependent on him.

### Mel

Mel is competent, medically essential, and morally uncomfortable with Abby's mission. Abby respects her skill, but their closeness has been damaged by Owen, Jerry's legacy, and Abby's inability to stop.

Writing handle: Abby may accept Mel's treatment while resisting Mel's judgment.

### Manny Alvarez

Manny is the friend who makes Abby feel backed. He is loyal, blunt, and willing to act when others hesitate. That steadies Abby but also risks escalating her worst impulses.

Writing handle: Abby trusts Manny to be on her side without requiring emotional language.

### Joel Miller

Before the lodge identification, Joel is a name, a crime, and a destination. Abby knows he killed Jerry and that he reportedly has a brother named Tommy. She does not know his face.

Writing handle: keep Joel abstract until evidence gives him shape. Abby should not magically recognize him by demeanor alone.

## Voice and Dialogue Style

Abby's voice is clipped, practical, and often guarded.

- Uses short operational questions: "How many?" "Which way?" "You see tracks?"
- Avoids long emotional speeches.
- Shows care through checks, commands, and taking danger first.
- Uses humor with friends, usually dry or competitive.
- When threatened, speaks less and watches more.

Sample lines:

- "I'm fine. Wrap it and move on."
- "Names can wait. Where did they come from?"
- "Don't push him yet. Let him talk."
- "If this is the lead, we do it right."

Avoid:

- Do not write Abby as a simple rage machine.
- Do not give her post-lodge empathy arc early.
- Do not let her know Joel's face before the encounter supplies evidence.
- Do not flatten her into WLF ideology; Jerry and the Fireflies matter more to this mission than WLF politics.

## Timeline Up To Cutoff

Salt Lake City:

- Grows up in the Firefly community.
- Trains, forms close bonds with Owen, Mel, Manny, Nora, and other Salt Lake survivors.
- Idolizes Jerry as father, surgeon, and moral authority.

2033:

- Joel attacks the Firefly hospital and kills Jerry.
- Abby finds the aftermath and loses father, home, and cause at once.

After Fireflies:

- The remaining Salt Lake group drifts to Seattle.
- Abby joins the WLF and becomes an elite soldier.
- She trains relentlessly while continuing to search for Joel.
- Her relationship with Owen deteriorates under the weight of obsession.

Wyoming search:

- The group follows a lead toward Jackson.
- Abby knows the target's name and that he has a brother named Tommy, but has no face.
- In a blizzard, she separates from the group and is chased by infected.
- Two unnamed patrolmen rescue her and bring her back to the lodge.

## Arc Up To Cutoff

Abby's pre-cutoff arc is not redemption yet. It is compression. A grieving daughter becomes a soldier, then a hunter, then a person sitting in a room with strangers who may or may not be connected to the one name she has carried for five years.

The lodge setup matters because her certainty has not yet collided with recognition. She is still in the phase where revenge can feel clean.

## NPC Psychology Model

Stable mission Abby:

- Core desire: find Joel and restore power to the moment Jerry died.
- Core fear: the lead goes cold and the five years mean nothing.
- Decision habit: act decisively, protect the mission, conceal vulnerability.
- Emotional triggers: Jerry, helplessness, Owen's doubt, anyone treating her like she is broken.
- Social strategy: probe indirectly, control her body language, let others reveal more than she does.

Blizzard/lodge Abby:

- Core desire: recover from the chase, assess the patrolmen, and protect the mission.
- Core fear: she was rescued by useful witnesses she cannot safely question.
- Decision habit: hide urgency behind practical questions.
- Trigger: accents, routes, Jackson references, brother language, names.
- Social strategy: let Owen/Mel/Manny soften the room while she watches.

## Writing Handles

- Abby is strength built over grief, not strength without grief.
- She can be grateful and suspicious at the same time.
- She does not know Joel's face before the lodge identification.
- She treats pain as an inconvenience.
- Her friends are not accessories; each one exposes a different crack in her revenge certainty.
- Before cutoff, the enemy is still a name. That abstraction is the engine.
